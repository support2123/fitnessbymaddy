Fat loss is not a cardio-vs-weights argument.

There is no science-backed “80% diet / 20% workout” split that fits everyone. Food usually makes a calorie deficit easier to create. Training raises energy use, helps preserve lean mass and strength, improves fitness, and helps make the plan more maintainable.

The evidence-led setup:
• Build aerobic volume: 150 minutes/week is a meaningful evidence-based starting point; more volume up to 300 minutes can add benefit.
• Lift while you diet: the scale is not the only outcome—protect lean mass and strength.
• HIIT is an option, not a shortcut. If steady cardio is easier to repeat, it is still a powerful fat-loss tool.
• Track trends over weeks, not one morning weigh-in.

Sources: Jayedi et al., JAMA Network Open 2024; resistance-training systematic review/meta-analysis 2025; WHO Physical Activity Guidelines 2020; NIDDK weight-management resources.

Pregnancy, diabetes medications, injury, eating-disorder history, or medical conditions deserve individualized support.
