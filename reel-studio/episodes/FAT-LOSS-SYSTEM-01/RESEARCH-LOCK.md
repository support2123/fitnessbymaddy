# FAT-LOSS-SYSTEM-01 — research lock

## Core conclusions

1. **There is no evidence-based universal “diet X% / exercise Y%” allocation.** Energy intake and expenditure interact dynamically and individual response/compensation differs. Use the practical hierarchy instead: dietary change generally makes an energy deficit easier to create; physical activity adds expenditure and delivers independent fitness/health, body-composition, and maintenance benefits. Do not display “80/20,” “90/10,” or any universal split.
2. **For direct body-weight, waist, and fat reduction, accumulated aerobic exercise has the clearest dose-response evidence.** A 2024 JAMA Network Open systematic review/meta-analysis of 116 RCTs / 6,880 adults with overweight or obesity found increasing moderate-to-vigorous aerobic duration up to 300 min/week was associated with progressively lower weight, waist, and fat measures. At 150 min/week: estimated −2.79 kg body weight; at 300 min/week: −4.19 kg. These are group averages against control, not individual promises. ≥150 min/week was associated with clinically important waist and body-fat reductions.
3. **HIIT is not a universal fat-loss winner.** A systematic review/meta-analysis in adults with overweight/obesity found no significant HIIT-versus-MICT difference for weight, BMI, waist, body-fat percentage, or fat mass; HIIT had an insulin-sensitivity advantage in that review. Present it as a time-efficient option, not a metabolic loophole.
4. **Resistance training is a body-composition safeguard in a calorie deficit.** A 2025 systematic review/meta-analysis of dietary weight-loss interventions found resistance exercise did not change total body-mass loss but produced greater fat-mass loss, preserved fat-free mass, and increased strength. Therefore: do not call lifting “the best calorie burner”; call it essential to preserving the tissue and capability you want to keep.
5. **Combined, sustainable approaches beat false either/or framing.** Long-term programs combining diet, activity, and behavior support tend to produce more favorable outcomes than single components. A practical general-adult start is 150 minutes moderate activity weekly + two strength days, then progress based on recovery, preference, and clinical context.

## Claims not allowed

- “HIIT burns more fat than all other cardio.”
- Any “afterburn” claim as the main explanation for fat loss.
- “Spot reduce belly fat.”
- “Diet is exactly X% and workout is exactly Y%.”
- Universal calorie targets, protein targets, step targets, or speed-of-loss prescriptions.
- Promises of a specific body or outcome.

## Safety boundary

General education only. Add visual/caption safety line for pregnancy, diabetes/medications, injury, eating-disorder history, or other health conditions: individual plan with a qualified clinician/dietitian/trainer may be appropriate.
