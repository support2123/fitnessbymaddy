# FATLOSS-02 — Research lock and claim boundaries

**Review date:** 2 October 2026  
**Audience:** general adult population. This is education, not medical treatment.

## Editorial answer in one sentence

There is **no evidence-based fixed percentage** such as “80% diet / 20% workout.” A caloric deficit is individual and changes over time; diet is often the easier initial lever, while aerobic activity raises expenditure, resistance training protects lean mass/body composition, and both improve the chance of a durable result.

## Approved statements for narration and graphics

| Statement | Evidence / boundary | On-screen source chip |
|---|---|---|
| No fixed diet-versus-exercise percentage exists. | The percentage depends on the actual intake and expenditure changes for that person; it is not a biological constant. ACSM explicitly calls the 80/20 framing a myth. | `ACSM · WEIGHT-LOSS MYTHBUSTING · 2025` |
| 150–250 min/wk of moderate activity generally produces modest loss / helps prevent gain; greater volumes are associated with more clinically meaningful loss and post-loss maintenance. | Dose-response guideline language; not a personal guarantee. | `DONNELLY / ACSM · MSSE · 2009` |
| Aerobic exercise is the most direct exercise tool for energy expenditure; resistance training is especially valuable during a deficit for body composition. | An overview of 12 systematic reviews found aerobic training superior for weight/fat/visceral-fat loss; resistance training most effective for preserving lean mass during diet-induced loss. | `BELLICHA · OBESITY REVIEWS · 2021` |
| Resistance exercise during dietary loss preserves fat-free mass and increases fat-mass loss compared with diet alone. | 2025 SR/MA: 25 RCTs, 1,608 participants; no significant extra *body-mass* change, but favourable fat-mass and fat-free-mass outcomes. | `BINMAHFOZ · BMJ OPEN SPORT EX MED · 2025` |
| HIIT is not a “magic” fat-loss method. | In 13 trials, HIIT and MICT both reduced fat mass/waist with no significant body-composition difference; HIIT required ~40% less session time. Later reviews are mixed, so script makes no superiority claim. | `WEWEGE · OBESITY REVIEWS · 2017` |
| A portion of added exercise energy may be compensated for. | Careau et al. examined data from 1,754 adults and estimated average energy compensation of 28%. This is a population estimate, not an individual calorie correction. | `CAREAU · CURRENT BIOLOGY · 2021` |
| Diet plus physical activity tends to outperform diet alone over longer follow-up and substantially outperforms activity-only programs for weight loss. | Johns et al. meta-analysis: combined versus diet-only −1.72 kg at 12 months; combined versus activity-only −5.33 kg at 3–6 months. Results and interventions are heterogeneous. | `JOHNS · J ACAD NUTR DIET · 2014` |
| Insufficient sleep can harm fat-loss partitioning. | Small controlled crossover study (n=10) during matched calorie restriction: 5.5 h vs 8.5 h sleep opportunity was associated with 55% less fat loss. Do not present this as a precise effect for every person. | `NEDELTCHEVA · ANN INTERN MED · 2010` |
| ~1.6 g/kg/day protein is a useful reference for many resistance-trained healthy adults. | Meta-regression of protein supplementation + resistance training found no further mean benefit above ~1.62 g/kg/day for fat-free mass gain. This is not a disease-specific prescription and is not a fat-loss trial. | `MORTON · BJSM · 2018` |
| Spot reduction is not supported. | Six weeks of abdominal exercise alone improved endurance but did not significantly reduce abdominal fat measures in a small RCT. | `VISPUTE · JSCR · 2011` |

## Claims deliberately excluded

- “Spot reduction” from a particular workout.
- A universal calorie target, daily step count, or diet/workout percentage.
- A promise that HIIT burns more fat than steady cardio.
- “Afterburn” as a major fat-loss driver.
- Fasting, supplements, sweat, or detoxes as fat-loss requirements.
- Any statement that a scale plateau proves a damaged metabolism.
