# FATLOSS-02 — Decisions before picture lock

## Core answer to the creator brief

**Which workout helps most with fat loss?**

- For the largest direct exercise-driven energy expenditure: **aerobic work** that the person can accumulate and recover from.
- For time efficiency: **HIIT can be useful**, but it is not automatically superior to moderate continuous cardio for fat loss.
- For preserving muscle and improving body composition while dieting: **resistance training** is essential.
- For the best practical result: **a combined system**—food structure + strength training + aerobic movement + recovery.

## The percentage answer

Never say “diet is X% and workout is Y%.” There is no universal study-supported ratio. Show a clearly-labelled **illustrative deficit ledger**, not a claim:

`Example: −300 kcal/day from intake + −100 kcal/day from movement = 400 kcal/day deficit.`

In that *example*, movement supplies 25% of the mathematical deficit. In another real person, the share could be much smaller or larger. The purpose is to replace a viral slogan with a measurable framework.

## Recommended protocol shown in the film

1. Establish a modest, sustainable energy deficit with food structure.
2. Resistance train 2–4 days/week, scaled to experience and recovery.
3. Accumulate 150–300 min/week moderate aerobic activity (or vigorous equivalent), then progress as appropriate.
4. Increase daily movement from personal baseline; avoid one universal step prescription.
5. Use intervals as an optional 0–2 sessions/week tool only when they are tolerated and do not degrade strength/recovery.
6. Monitor weekly average body mass, waist, performance, appetite, sleep, and adherence—not just a single weigh-in.

## Safety

The final video must include a concise medical qualifier. Individual programming is mandatory for pregnancy/postpartum, injuries, cardiovascular symptoms, diabetes medication, kidney disease, eating-disorder history, very low body weight, or unexplained changes in weight.
