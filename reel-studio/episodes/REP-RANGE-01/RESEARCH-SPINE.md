# REP-RANGE-01 — approved research spine

This record locks only the claims approved for this episode. It preserves the operator-provided bounds rather than treating a group average as an individual promise.

1. **Thirty percent, to failure, built as much muscle as eighty percent** — Mitchell, *Journal of Applied Physiology*, 2012. Untrained men; ten weeks; leg extension; MRI. Use “as much as,” never “identical.”
2. **Eight to twelve became the textbook hypertrophy zone** — ACSM Position Stand, 2009. It is efficient, not wrong.
3. **Twenty eight studies and seven hundred forty seven people found similar muscle growth across light, moderate, and heavy loading to failure** — Lopez, *Medicine and Science in Sports and Exercise*, 2021. Similar group averages.
4. **Small fibres recruit first; large high-threshold fibres join as a set gets hard** — Henneman, *Science*, 1957. Mechanism, not a displayed performance statistic.
5. **At thirty percent, failure grew more than stopping short; at eighty percent, failure added nothing** — Lasevicius, *Journal of Strength and Conditioning Research*, 2022. Twenty five untrained men; eight weeks.
6. **Approximately twenty percent was the weakest loading condition for growth** — Lasevicius, *European Journal of Sport Science*, 2018. It still produced some growth.
7. **Hypertrophy increases as sets end nearer failure; strength is far less dependent on proximity to failure** — Robinson, *Sports Medicine*, 2024.
8. **One to six heavy reps build the most maximal strength** — Lopez, *Medicine and Science in Sports and Exercise*, 2021; Schoenfeld et al., 2017.
9. **Fifteen to thirty plus reps build local muscular endurance** — Schoenfeld et al., *Sports*, 2021. This is not a cardio claim.
10. **Twenty percent bar-speed loss produced similar strength and better jump outcomes than forty percent loss** — Pareja-Blanco, *Scandinavian Journal of Medicine and Science in Sports*, 2017. Full squat; eight weeks.
11. **Spine bone density changed plus two point nine percent versus minus one point two percent in the lower-intensity group** — Watson, *Journal of Bone and Mineral Research*, 2018. Postmenopausal women with low bone mass; eight months; supervised.
12. **Tendons adapt to high loads, not light loads** — Bohm, *Sports Medicine Open*, 2015. Meta-analysis in healthy adults.
13. **In one programme, five hundred eighty five people ranged from minus two to plus fifty nine percent muscle growth** — Hubal, *Medicine and Science in Sports and Exercise*, 2005. Twelve-week arm training; individual response range.
14. **Ten to twenty hard sets per muscle per week is the productive zone** — Schoenfeld, *Journal of Sports Sciences*, 2017; Baz-Valle, *Journal of Human Kinetics*, 2022. Returns diminish above roughly twenty.

## Excluded claims

The long research brief also documents rest, tempo, blood-flow restriction, endocrine claims, endurance-athlete economy, and percentage-to-repetition variation. They are useful for later episodes but do not enter this locked script or on-screen statistical package.
