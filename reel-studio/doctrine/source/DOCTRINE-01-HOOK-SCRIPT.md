# HOOK + SCRIPT DOCTRINE — v1

Master scripting spec for the DECODE engine. Any fitness / health / body / mind topic. Copy the templates, fill the brackets, pass the checklist, ship. Every worked example already obeys the line-craft and citation rules so they can be lifted as-is.

**Two output layers, kept separate in every script:**
- **ON-SCREEN** = kinetic text / stat cards. Digits allowed. Big. Citation attached.
- **SPOKEN** = the voice line. Numbers spelled out for cadence. Periods and commas only.

**Standing constraints (never break):** English only. Secular. The two-letter term for machine intelligence never appears on screen or in caption. Every statistic carries author plus year on screen. Thin evidence is bounded out loud. No medical promise. No guru tone.

---

## 1 · HOOK SYSTEM

**The Hook Law.** In the first three seconds the viewer must (a) instantly know what the video is about *as a category* and (b) be structurally unable to scroll because a gap has been opened that only watching closes. A hook that does only (a) is a label. A hook that does only (b) is clickbait and dies at second four. It must do both.

**Hook = ON-SCREEN line + SPOKEN line firing together.** They are not the same sentence. On-screen is the freeze-frame the thumb catches. Spoken is the momentum that carries into second four.

### NAME vs TEASE — the mix

Decide per reel, then balance across the slate.

| Situation | Do this |
|---|---|
| Topic is itself the desire (belly fat, protein, sleep, grip, energy) | **NAME it.** The word is the hook. Withholding kills reach because search and intent live in the word. |
| Topic sounds boring but the consequence is dramatic (fascia, resting heart rate, breathing rate) | **TEASE the consequence, category still clear.** Promise the payoff, not the noun. |
| The number is the shock | **Lead with the number, name the topic in line two.** |
| You have made 3+ reels naming the topic head-on | **Rotate to a tease** to break pattern fatigue in the feed. |

**Rules for a tease:** the category must still be graspable by second three (viewer knows this is a body / training / sleep / nutrition video, just not the exact lever). Never withhold past second three. A tease still promises a specific payoff, never "watch to find out."

**Slate ratio target:** roughly six named to four teased across any ten reels. All-named = feed goes flat. All-teased = reach drops and saves fall.

### The 12 archetypes

Each: TEMPLATE (fill the brackets) + one worked fitness example (ON-SCREEN / SPOKEN).

**1. The Checkable Contradiction** — a self-test the viewer runs in their own head and loses.
- TEMPLATE: `You do [common action] to get [goal]. [Action] is what blocks [goal].`
- ON-SCREEN: `Stretching before you lift makes you WEAKER.`
- SPOKEN: `You stretch to lift heavier. Static stretching before a heavy set drops your force output for the next ten minutes.`

**2. The Forbidden Number** — a figure that feels like it must be wrong.
- TEMPLATE: `[Number]. That is [what it counts], and it is [smaller / larger] than you think.`
- ON-SCREEN: `1.6 g/kg` · citation card `Morton et al., Br J Sports Med, 2018`
- SPOKEN: `One point six grams per kilo. Above that, extra protein builds almost no extra muscle. Most people are eating for a body twice their size.`

**3. The Body-Horror Stat** — a visceral internal consequence you can almost feel.
- TEMPLATE: `Every [time unit] you [behavior], [internal thing] [does the grim thing].`
- ON-SCREEN: `After 30, you lose muscle every year you do nothing.`
- SPOKEN: `From your thirties, you lose three to eight percent of your muscle every decade if you never train. Your body is quietly returning the engine you were born with.`

**4. You've Been Sold a Lie** — attack a claim the industry profits from. Name the seller, not a person.
- TEMPLATE: `The [product / rule] that promised [benefit] was built to [real motive], not to [benefit].`
- ON-SCREEN: `"Detox teas" do one thing. Make you poop.`
- SPOKEN: `The detox tea sold you a cleaner body. It sells a laxative. Your liver already does the detox, for free, every second.`

**5. The Identity Call-Out** — name the exact person watching by their behavior.
- TEMPLATE: `If you [specific tell], your [body part / system] is already [state]. This is for you.`
- ON-SCREEN: `If you sit 8 hours a day, read this before you stand up.`
- SPOKEN: `If you sit for eight hours a day, the muscles that hold your hips are shortening while you scroll this.`

**6. The Impossible Comparison** — bind two unlike things with one number.
- TEMPLATE: `[Behavior] does the same [damage / benefit] to your [system] as [shocking equivalent].`
- ON-SCREEN: `Weak grip predicts death better than blood pressure.`
- SPOKEN: `Your grip strength predicts how long you will live more sharply than your blood pressure does.` · card `Leong et al., The Lancet, 2015`

**7. The Countdown / Deadline** — time-boxed stakes.
- TEMPLATE: `In [time], your [system] will [change]. You decide which direction.`
- ON-SCREEN: `Stop training. Your gains start leaving in 2 weeks.`
- SPOKEN: `Two weeks without training and your strength starts to fade. But here is the part nobody tells you about how fast it comes back.`

**8. The Reframe** — redefine a familiar thing so it can never be seen the old way.
- TEMPLATE: `[Familiar thing] is not [assumed category]. It is [true category].`
- ON-SCREEN: `Soreness is not progress. It is damage.`
- SPOKEN: `Soreness is not the workout working. It is tissue damage. You can grow with almost none of it.`

**9. The Cost Ledger** — one habit priced in a hard unit.
- TEMPLATE: `[Habit] is costing you [unit] of [thing you want], and you never see the bill.`
- ON-SCREEN: `Sleeping under 6 hours cut fat loss by more than half.`
- SPOKEN: `Sleeping less than six hours cut dieters fat loss by fifty five percent, and pushed the loss into muscle instead.` · card `Nedeltcheva et al., Ann Intern Med, 2010`

**10. The Named Enemy** — give the mechanism a villain name.
- TEMPLATE: `There is one reason [problem]. Its name is [mechanism, plain-English].`
- ON-SCREEN: `The reason your fat loss stalled has a name. Adaptation.`
- SPOKEN: `Your fat loss did not break. It adapted. Your body lowered its own burn rate to match the smaller you.`

**11. The Receipts Open** — start mid-proof, evidence first.
- TEMPLATE: `[Number] people. [One-line finding]. Here is what it means for your [thing].`
- ON-SCREEN: `39 people. Their muscle "remembered" training for years.`
- SPOKEN: `Thirty nine muscle samples. The muscle kept a memory of past training written into its cells. A small study, a real signal.` · card `Seaborne et al., Sci Reports, 2018`

**12. The Mirror Question** — a question they answer wrong in their head, then you correct on the beat.
- TEMPLATE: `How [much / long / often] [common variable]. Whatever you guessed, it is [wrong direction].`
- ON-SCREEN: `How long to build a visible muscle? Longer than one photo.`
- SPOKEN: `How long to build a muscle you can actually see. If you guessed weeks, the honest answer is months, and here is the timeline.`

## 2 · SCRIPT ARCHITECTURE — 3 TIERS

**Pick the tier by how much the topic can carry, not by ambition.** One idea = Tier A. A myth with a fix = Tier B. A full mechanism with tax, proof, lie and protocol = Tier C. Forcing a one-idea topic into 90 seconds is the most common way these die.

**Retention cadence law (all tiers):** a fresh turn, number, or visual break every eight to ten seconds. If the viewer can predict the next line, you have already lost them. Each beat must answer a *different* question than the beat before it.

**The angle grid (anti-bloat engine).** Six angles exist. Each answers one viewer question. One beat = one angle = one question. If two beats answer the same question, cut one.

| Angle | Viewer question it answers |
|---|---|
| TAX | What is ignoring this costing me? |
| MECHANISM | Why does this happen? |
| MYTH | What did I get wrong? |
| PROTOCOL | What do I do Monday? |
| PROOF | Why should I believe you? |
| IDENTITY | Is this me? |

Tier A uses one angle. Tier B uses three. Tier C uses five to six. That is the whole depth ladder.

---

### TIER A · 15–30s · single devastating idea

One angle. Usually MYTH or TAX. No chapters.

| Beat | Time | Job |
|---|---|---|
| HOOK | 0–3s | Archetype fires. Category clear, gap open. |
| PROOF | 3–10s | One cited stat. One card. |
| TURN | 10–18s | The flip. This is the whole reel. |
| PAYOFF | 18–26s | The one thing to do or know. |
| CTA | 26–30s | One owned-number comment trigger. |

- **The turn:** lands at ~second twelve. It is the reel. If there is no clean flip, this is not a Tier A topic.
- **Honesty-bound:** only if the single stat is thin. Placed in the same breath as the stat, never later.
- **CTA:** one line, one owned number (see CTA bank).

**Worked (Tier A):**
- HOOK ON-SCREEN `Soreness is not progress.` / SPOKEN `Soreness is not the workout working. It is damage.`
- PROOF SPOKEN `You can gain muscle with barely any soreness at all.`
- TURN SPOKEN `Chasing the ache just means you are under recovering. Sore does not mean stronger.`
- PAYOFF SPOKEN `Judge the session by the weight on the bar going up over weeks. Not by how wrecked you feel tomorrow.`
- CTA SPOKEN `Comment how sore you are today, zero to ten.`

---

### TIER B · 30–60s · myth with a fix

Three angles. Default sequence TAX → MECHANISM → PROTOCOL, or MYTH → PROOF → PROTOCOL. Light chapter cards.

| Beat | Time | Job |
|---|---|---|
| HOOK | 0–3s | Archetype fires. |
| THE TAX | 3–12s | Cost of ignoring. One number. |
| THE SYSTEM | 12–28s | Mechanism. Why it happens. |
| THE TURN | 28–40s | The lie or reframe. Peak of the reel. |
| THE PROTOCOL | 40–52s | Two or three concrete moves. |
| CTA | 52–60s | Owned-number trigger + series promise. |

- **Cadence:** four beats after the hook, ~nine seconds each. Every chapter card is a re-hook.
- **The turn:** at ~second thirty, the midpoint. Everything before sets the trap, everything after pays it.
- **Honesty-bound:** attached to whichever stat is thinnest, spoken in that beat.
- **CTA:** one owned number, then a one-line loop to the next reel.

**Worked (Tier B) — topic: sleep and fat loss**
- HOOK ON-SCREEN `Sleeping under 6 hours cut fat loss by more than half.` / SPOKEN `Sleeping less than six hours cut fat loss by fifty five percent.` card `Nedeltcheva et al., Ann Intern Med, 2010`
- TAX SPOKEN `Same diet. Same calories. The short sleepers lost the weight, but most of it came off as muscle, not fat.`
- SYSTEM SPOKEN `Short sleep raises hunger hormones and drops the ones that keep muscle. Your body defends fat and burns the engine.`
- TURN SPOKEN `Here is the honest bound. This was ten people in a lab. A strong signal, not a promise. But it points one clear way.`
- PROTOCOL SPOKEN `Fix three things. A fixed wake time. No screens for the last hour. A cool dark room. Sleep is a fat loss tool, not a reward.`
- CTA SPOKEN `Comment how many hours you slept last night. Follow, because next we break down the one nap length that helps.`

---

### TIER C · 60–120s · full multi-angle

Five to six angles as named chapters. This is the DECODE flagship shape. Each chapter card is a mini-hook and a scroll-reset.

**Canonical chapter order:** `THE TAX → THE SYSTEM → THE LIE → THE RECEIPT → THE PROTOCOL`. Add `THE COST` or `WHO` only if it answers a question none of the others do.

| Chapter | Time | Angle | Job |
|---|---|---|---|
| HOOK | 0–3s | — | Archetype fires. |
| THE TAX | 3–18s | TAX | What ignoring this costs. Open on a number. |
| THE SYSTEM | 18–38s | MECHANISM | The machinery. Simple diagram or metaphor. |
| THE LIE | 38–58s | MYTH | The reframe. **The turn of the whole reel.** |
| THE RECEIPT | 58–78s | PROOF | The cited study. Honesty-bound if thin. |
| THE PROTOCOL | 78–105s | PROTOCOL | Three to four moves, numbered. |
| CTA | 105–120s | — | Owned number + series loop. |

- **Cadence:** each chapter is ten to eighteen seconds and opens with its title card, which re-hooks the thumb. Never let a chapter run past eighteen seconds without a fresh number or visual.
- **The turn:** always in THE LIE, at roughly the midpoint. The first half builds the belief. THE LIE breaks it. The second half rebuilds correctly.
- **Honesty-bound placement:** inside THE RECEIPT, spoken in the same breath as the number. Format: `[n] people. A signal, not a promise.` This *raises* trust and is a save trigger, not a weakness.
- **CTA:** one owned number, one series loop line.
- **Every angle without bloat:** run the six-question grid. If THE TAX and THE COST both answer "what is it costing me," you have one chapter, not two. Cut to five. Ship lean.

**Worked (Tier C) — topic: grip strength and longevity**
- HOOK ON-SCREEN `Weak grip predicts death better than blood pressure.` / SPOKEN `Your grip strength predicts how long you will live more sharply than your blood pressure does.`
- THE TAX SPOKEN `In a study of nearly one hundred forty thousand adults, every five kilos of lost grip strength tracked with a sixteen percent higher risk of dying early.` card `Leong et al., The Lancet, 2015`
- THE SYSTEM SPOKEN `Grip is a window. It reads how much total muscle and nerve drive you have kept. Lose the grip, and you are usually losing the whole engine.`
- THE LIE SPOKEN `The lie is that grip trainers fix this. Squeezing a spring builds the spring skill. What moved the numbers was full body strength. Grip is the gauge, not the cause.`
- THE RECEIPT SPOKEN `This was an observational study across seventeen countries. It shows a link, it does not prove the arrow. A strong signal, read it honestly.`
- THE PROTOCOL SPOKEN `Train the gauge the right way. One. Carry heavy things, two walks a week. Two. Deadlift or row without straps. Three. Dead hangs, thirty seconds, three times.`
- CTA SPOKEN `Comment your age, and whether you can hang for thirty seconds. Follow, the next one is the walking pace that predicts the same thing.`

## 3 · THE VIRAL CHECKLIST — pass / fail

Run every finished script through all six. **Ship only at six of six.** A fail is a rewrite, not a discount.

| # | Axis | The literal test | Pass condition |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Shareable | Name one specific person a viewer sends this to, to win an argument or warn a friend. | A named person comes to mind in under two seconds. If not → fail. |
| 2 | Saveable | Point to the discrete thing worth returning to. | There is a numbered protocol or a single number worth saving. Vague advice → fail. |
| 3 | Commentable | Name the one-token reply the viewer drops. | An owned number, or a clean two-camp yes/no, is explicitly asked. No prompt → fail. |
| 4 | Rewatch | Point to the one beat that flashed too fast to fully catch. | Exactly one deliberate catch-me number or line exists. Everything catchable first pass → fail. |
| 5 | Screenshot | Point to the single frame that stands alone as a stat card with citation. | One frame = claim plus author plus year, readable with sound off. None → fail. |
| 6 | Wait-for-next | Read the last two seconds. Does it open a loop the next reel closes? | The CTA names or promises the next topic. Dead-ends → fail. |

**Sound-off gate (pre-check, mandatory):** the hook and the screenshot frame must both land with zero audio. If the reel only works with sound, it fails before it reaches the six.

## 4 · LINE-CRAFT RULES

**Sentence shape.** Short. Declarative. One idea per line. If a line has two ideas, it is two lines. Read every SPOKEN line out loud. If you run out of breath, cut it.

**Number-first.** Open lines and cards on the number, not the setup. `Fifty five percent less fat` beats `A study found that fat loss was reduced`.

**Zero hype.** Banned words: literally, insane, crazy, mind-blowing, game-changer, secret, hack, magic, revolutionary, shocking, unbelievable, this changes everything, science-backed (show the citation instead of claiming it). The number is the drama. Adjectives dilute it.

**TTS-safe punctuation.** Periods and commas only. No dashes, no ellipses, no semicolons, no colons inside spoken lines, no parentheses. Where you would pause with a dash, start a new sentence.

**Numbers, two layers.**
- ON-SCREEN: digits. `1.6 g/kg`. `55%`. `8,000 steps`.
- SPOKEN: spell them for cadence. `One point six grams per kilo`. `Fifty five percent`. `Eight thousand steps`. This is what gives the voice its rhythm and stops it rushing a digit.

**Avoid "under."** It reads weak and clips in voice. Use `below` or `less than`. `Below seven hours`, `less than six hours`. Never `under seven hours`.

**Citations.** On-screen format: `Author et al., Journal, Year`. Every stat, every time. No exceptions. Verify the exact figure and year against the source before it airs. A wrong number in a science channel is a credibility wound.

**Honesty-bound phrasing.** When evidence is thin, bound it in the same breath, out loud, as a feature. `Thirty nine people. A signal, not a promise.` `One lab, ten subjects. Read it honestly.` Never bury the caveat and never let a small study wear the voice of settled fact.

**Rewrites (bad → good):**
- `This insane hack will literally transform your gains overnight` → `Two weeks off, and your strength starts to fade. Here is how fast it returns.`
- `Studies show protein is really important for muscle` → `One point six grams per kilo. Above that, extra protein builds almost no extra muscle.`
- `You won't believe what sitting does to your body` → `Sit for eight hours a day, and your hip muscles shorten while you scroll this.`
