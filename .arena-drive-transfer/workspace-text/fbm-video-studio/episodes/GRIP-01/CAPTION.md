## CAPTION (copy-paste, 1,480 characters incl. line breaks)

Your hands are the last thing you notice and the first thing that leaves.

In 2015, The Lancet followed 139,691 adults across 17 countries for four years. They measured one thing with a cheap little dynamometer: how hard you can squeeze.

Every 5 kg less grip came with a 16% higher risk of dying. That number held up in a second study of half a million people, where grip beat blood pressure and even total physical activity as a predictor.

Nobody is saying a weak hand kills you. It is a marker, not a verdict. A body that stops gripping is a body that has stopped being asked to grip.

So ask it something. Sit to stand, arms crossed, for thirty seconds. Count every stand. If you are over sixty, twelve is the line for a woman and fourteen for a man.

Then comment your number. I read them.

If you want me to build your week around exactly what your body is doing right now, the free scan is in my bio. Eleven questions, three minutes, no card. I build the plan by hand, and it lands in twelve hours.
