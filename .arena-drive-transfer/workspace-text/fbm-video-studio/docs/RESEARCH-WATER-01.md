## The spine (what we decided to say)
The deepest true thing about water in a training body is **not** "drink more" — it is that **every beat of your life is a fluid decision**. So the film is built on a rate, not a rule:

1. **Your heart moves ~7,500 L of fluid a day while you hold only ~5 L at any moment.** (cardiac output ≈ 5 L/min, Guyton & Hall). Reframe: you are a fleet, not a tank.
2. **You are ~60 % water ≈ 42 L at 70 kg** (Buono & Kolkhorst; Gray's). Two thirds intracellular; brain ~73 %.
3. **THE DATUM:** for every **1 % of body water lost**, heart rate climbs about **+3 bpm** at the same pace — same work, more beats (Adams et al. 2014 systematic review; Montain & Coyle 1992).
4. **THE HONESTY BEAT (this is the brand):** the famous "2 % rule" **came from the lab, not the road**. In real time-trial conditions, dehydration up to ~4 % body mass did **not** impair performance (Goulet 2012 meta-analysis of 28 effect estimates; Cheuvront & Kenefick 2014). We ship the correction to our own genre — the confusions we exploit are the ones we name.
5. **THE MYTH + THE DANGER:** *"8 glasses a day"* has **no scientific basis** — it traces to a 1945 US Food & Nutrition Board line that most of the day's water comes **from food**, read halfway (Valtin 2002, AJP). Worse: past thirst, plain water **outruns sodium** — exercise-associated hyponatraemia (EAH) has killed **at least 14 athletes since 1981**, and the 2015 consensus's prevention advice is literally **"drink to thirst, not to a number"** (Hew-Butler et al. 2015, Clin J Sport Med / BJSM).
6. **THE PROOF:** your kidneys filter **~180 L/day** (GFR 125 mL/min) and return **~99 %** to you — thirst is that system's own alarm, not a weakness (Guyton & Hall).
7. **THE PROTOCOL:** weigh in, weigh out — **1 kg lost = 1 L**; replace **1.2–1.5 ×** the loss over the next ~3 h; keep losses ≤ 2 % (≈ **1.4 kg at 70 kg**).
8. **THE RECEIPT:** cramps were **never proven** to be a sodium problem — across a 161 km ultramarathon, blood sodium did not separate crampers from non-crampers (Hoffman & Stuempfle 2015). Fix fluid, load, sleep and pacing first.

## Held in reserve (researched, not spoken — keeps the runtime lean)
- **Brain clearance:** glymphatic flow rises ~60 % and Aβ clearance ~2× in sleep (Xie/Nedergaard 2013, Science) — mechanism is mouse-based; keep for a sleep episode where it can carry its limits.
- **Muscle water:** ~3 g water bound per 1 g glycogen, higher with full rehydration (Fernández-Elías 2015) — explains "why the scale moved 1.5 kg after a carb day". Reserve for the muscle/scale topic.
- **Sweat rates:** 0.3–2.4 L/h typical, elite up to ~4.5 g sodium/h (0.6–2.6 L/h range; sweat Na 15–103 mmol/L). Good for a deep-dive; too wide for a 2-minute film.
- **Hyperthermia:** core temp +0.15–0.22 °C per 1 % loss (Sawka/Huggins). Could replace the bpm stat on a heat-specific cutdown.
- **Glycogen/plasma:** plasma volume falls 5–15 % at 2–6 % hypohydration (Cheuvront 2010) — mechanism slide for a carousel.
- **Boron/renal replacement:** kidneys return ~99 % of filtrate — could be a story of its own.

## Claims we deliberately did NOT make (guardrails)
- ❌ "Dehydration kills your gains" — unsupported at the magnitudes normal lifters reach.
- ❌ "You need 3–4 L a day" — no universal number; 8×8's ghost would just re-enter as a new number.
- ❌ "Salt your water to avoid cramps" — Hoffman 2015 says the evidence isn't there.
- ❌ Any mortality framing — this episode has no observational cohort claim, so no honesty chip for correlation is needed (the only chips are the road-vs-lab and cramps corrections).
- ❌ "Detox" language of any kind; the kidneys do the job, and we say exactly how.
