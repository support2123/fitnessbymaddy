### Retention Architecture: Open Loop → Payoff

A hook that stops the scroll but doesn't hold = a wasted stop. The 30-second reel is engineered as a **loop stack**, not a lecture.

**The FBM 5-beat spine (fits the five-systems brand):**

| Beat | Seconds | Job | Device |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1. The Stop** | 0–1.5 | Fire one of the three reflexes | Hook formula + number on screen |
| **2. The Stakes** | 1.5–5 | Tell them why this matters to *their* body/goal | "And here's why that changes how you should train." |
| **3. The Reveal** | 5–18 | Pay off the hook — the mechanism, the number, the demo | Anatomy render / on-body proof + on-screen citation |
| **4. The Turn** | 18–25 | Connect the science to a decision they can make | "So the real question isn't X, it's Y." |
| **5. The Loop-Close + Soft CTA** | 25–30 | Close the opened loop, seed the next reel or the Body-Scan | "That's system one of five. Find out which of your five is holding you back → Body-Scan in bio." |

**Loop mechanics that raise average-view-time:**
- **Nested loops:** open a second small loop inside the reveal ("but there's a catch…") so the brain never gets to "I've got it, I can leave."
- **Delayed number:** state the claim in the hook, deliver the *exact* figure only at the reveal — the missing digit is the leash.
- **The rehook at ~50%:** the mid-point is where most drop-off happens. Plant a pattern-break there — a cut to a new visual, a "wait" caption, a second stat. ("Half of you have already scrolled. The rest are about to learn why your recovery is broken.")
- **The end-is-the-beginning:** last line answers a question the first line asked, so the loop *audibly* closes — this is what triggers rewatches (and rewatches are the strongest ranking signal on Reels).
- **Caption as the extra room:** the on-screen hook is the trigger; the written caption carries the full citation, the second example, and the CTA. Two surfaces, two jobs.

**Series architecture (the compounding layer):** MADE OF FIVE is not one film — it's a **5-part loop machine.** Each part ends on "system [N] of 5 → next up: [teaser]." The whole series is one giant open loop, and the Body-Scan quiz is the payoff for the person who wants to know *which of their own five* is weakest. That converts a viewer into a lead without a single hard sell.

### 8.1 · Citation Discipline — "The Receipt"

The signature visual device of the whole page: **The Receipt.** Every quantitative or mechanistic claim wears its source in the lower-third — **Author, Year, Source** — held on screen ≥2 seconds, legible at thumbnail size. This is the *MADE OF FIVE* template energy (Kandel / West / Buono & Kolkhorst / Gray's on screen) scaled to daily content. No receipt, no claim.

**On-screen citation format (locked):**
`SCHOENFELD ET AL. 2017 · J Strength Cond Res` — never "studies show," never "science says." Name the human. Naming the researcher is what separates a teacher from an influencer.

**The Authority Map — who we cite for what (so we never mis-attribute):**

| Domain | Go-to authority | What they own |
|---|---|---|
| Hypertrophy, volume, rep ranges | **Brad Schoenfeld** | Volume dose-response (2017), mechanisms of hypertrophy (2010), load meta-analyses |
| Natural physique & protein | **Eric Helms** | Muscle & Strength Pyramids; Natural Bodybuilding recs (JISSN 2014) |
| Glutes & lower-body biomechanics | **Bret Contreras** | Hip thrust EMG, glute mechanics |
| Protein dose & MPS | **Stuart Phillips**; **Morton et al. 2018 (BJSM)** | Plateau ~1.6 g/kg/day; leucine threshold |
| Nutrient timing / "the window" | **Aragon & Schoenfeld 2013 (JISSN)** | Anabolic window myth-bust |
| Metabolic adaptation | **Fothergill 2016 (Obesity)**; **Trexler 2014 (JISSN)** | Biggest Loser; adaptive thermogenesis |
| Neuroscience / the synapse | **Eric Kandel — Principles of Neural Science** | ~20 nm synaptic cleft |
| Respiratory physiology / the lung | **John B. West — Respiratory Physiology** | Blood-gas barrier thickness |
| Thermoregulation / body water | **Buono & Kolkhorst** | Sweat, fluid balance |
| Anatomy / the frame | **Gray's Anatomy** | 206 bones, calcium stores |
| Supplements | **ISSN Position Stands** (Kreider 2017 creatine; Jäger 2017 protein) | Only creatine + protein are strongly supported |
| Guidelines-level truth | **ACSM · NSCA · NASM · Cochrane** | Position stands = Tier-1 evidence |

**Rule:** cite the *tightest* authority available. A position stand (ISSN, ACSM) or meta-analysis beats a single study; a single RCT beats a mechanism; a mechanism beats an opinion. If all we have is opinion, we say "my read, not settled science."

### 8.2 · The Secular-Not-Woo Guardrail (protecting the five-element lens)

The five elements are a **mnemonic scaffold welded to measured physiology** — a memory device, never a mechanism. The moment an element word explains *how the body works*, it becomes woo and a rival wins. Here is exactly how we keep the poetry and delete the mysticism.

**The One-Swap Test (apply to every script line):**
> Replace the element word with the organ/system name. If the sentence loses **zero** meaning, it's secular — keep it. If the element word was doing explanatory work (carrying a claim the organ name can't), **cut it.**
> - ✅ "SPACE — the synapse. A 20 nm gap your every thought jumps." (swap "SPACE"→"the synapse": meaning intact. Element is just a title.)
> - ❌ "Balance your Space element to think clearly." (swap fails — the word is claiming a mechanism. Woo. Delete.)

**Element → Real system → What we actually measure (the whole lens on one card):**

| Element (title only) | Real system | The measured number + receipt |
|---|---|---|
| SPACE | The synapse | ~20 nm cleft (Kandel) |
| AIR | The lung | ~12 breaths/min rest; ~0.3 µm barrier (West) |
| FIRE | The cell | ATP recycled ≈ bodyweight/day (physiology text) |
| WATER | Body fluid | ~60% of mass; ~42 L/70 kg; brain ~73% (Buono & Kolkhorst) |
| EARTH | The skeleton | 206 bones; ~99% of body calcium (Gray's) |

**The Forbidden Lexicon — banned from every client-facing frame, caption, and voiceover:**
`tattva · chakra · prana · aura · vibration · frequency (in the spiritual sense) · energy (as life-force) · cosmic · sacred · divine · alignment (spiritual) · balance your elements · dosha · healing energy · "the universe" · resonance`

**The Allowed reframe** (when a woo word tempts you, swap it):

| Woo temptation | Secular replacement |
|---|---|
| "raise your energy" | "raise your work capacity / VO₂ / drive" |
| "balance your body" | "restore joint symmetry / autonomic recovery" |
| "cleanse / detox" | "your liver and kidneys already clear it — here's the data" |
| "mind-body connection" | "the stress axis — cortisol, HRV, sleep architecture" |
| "flow / alignment" | "movement mechanics / posture under load" |

**The rule of placement:** poetry lives in the **title card and the transitions**. The **mechanism is 100% anatomy with a number and a name on it.** Element word appears at most **once** per piece — as the label — and never inside a claim.

### 8.3 · Accuracy Rules — "The Numbers Registry" & One Reference Body

Credibility dies from *internal contradiction* faster than from being wrong. If body water is "60%" in Monday's reel and "70%" in Thursday's, both are now suspect. We fix this with infrastructure.

**One Reference Body (stated once, pinned):** all general physiology numbers assume a **70 kg adult male** unless the topic is female-specific, in which case we state a **60 kg female reference** *and* flag the physiological difference (e.g., total body water ~50–55% vs ~60%; ~10–15× less testosterone). Never mix reference bodies inside one asset.

**The Numbers Registry** — a single source-of-truth doc every scriptwriter pulls from. Each recurring number lives here once, with its citation, and every asset uses the identical figure:

| Claim | Locked value | Receipt / note |
|---|---|---|
| Total body water | ~60% (~42 L / 70 kg) | Buono & Kolkhorst |
| Brain water | ~73% | Mitchell 1945, widely cited |
| Synaptic cleft | ~20 nm | Kandel |
| Resting breathing | ~12/min | clinical 12–20; we hold 12 |
| Blood-gas barrier | ~0.3 µm (thinnest side) | West — *see worked example below* |
| Bones (adult) | 206 | Gray's |
| Body calcium in bone/teeth | ~99% | Gray's |
| Protein for hypertrophy | 1.6–2.2 g/kg/day | Morton 2018; ISSN |
| Testosterone, M vs F | ~10–15× higher in men | endocrine reference ranges |
| Creatine dose | 3–5 g/day monohydrate | Kreider 2017 (ISSN) |

**Worked example of the discipline (use this internally as the training case):** the *MADE OF FIVE* film cited the lung barrier as **0.6 µm**; West's classic figure for the *thin gas-exchange side* is **~0.3 µm** (the ~0.6 µm is the thicker total tissue side). Both are technically defensible — which is exactly the problem. **We pick ONE** ("~0.3 µm at its thinnest, West") and hold it across every asset forever. Two defensible numbers used inconsistently reads as sloppy; one number used everywhere reads as authority.

**Framing verbs (the hedge discipline):**
- Approximations get **"about / roughly / ~"**: *"about 60% water."* Never present an estimate as exact.
- Exact facts get bare verbs: *"206 bones."*
- Ban absolute verbs on probabilistic claims: **no** "guarantees," "cures," "detoxes," "melts fat," "resets your metabolism."
- Correct verbs by evidence tier (below).

**Confidence Tiers (say the claim at its true strength):**
- **Tier 1** — position stands / meta-analyses → state as **fact**: *"Protein above ~1.6 g/kg stops adding muscle (Morton 2018)."*
- **Tier 2** — single RCT → **"one study found…"**
- **Tier 3** — mechanism / animal / textbook estimate → **"mechanistically / plausibly"** (this is where the ATP-≈-bodyweight figure lives: a textbook estimate, hedged, not stated as a hard measurement).

## TIER 0 — Brand-integrity cracks (fix before anything ships)

**0.1 · The "Five-Systems Spine" is Ayurveda in a lab coat. This is the whole moat cracking.**
SPACE / AIR / FIRE / WATER / EARTH are the *pancha mahabhuta* — the five tattvas of Ayurveda. You built an anti-woo brand whose entire organizing metaphor IS the classical five elements, then wrote a guardrail ("The Five Kept Honest") banning tattva/chakra/aura/energy/cosmic. A misinformation-burned skeptic — and every rival trainer/physio in the amplifier ring — recognizes this in one frame and screenshots it with "the 'no woo' guy organizes the body by earth/air/fire/water 😂." It also violates FBM's own Rule 02 (bans the five-element spiritual framing on client surfaces).
**Fix:** Kill the elemental names on-surface. Use plain system names: **NEURAL · RESPIRATORY · METABOLIC · FLUID · STRUCTURAL** (or NERVE/LUNG/CELL/BLOOD/FRAME). Keep the 5-part loop machine and the écorché visuals — just strip the mythic labels. If you love the pentad for internal production taxonomy, fine, but it must never surface with elemental branding. This one change is non-negotiable for the positioning to survive contact with the target audience.

**0.2 · Mortality hooks commit correlation = causation (physician-repellent).**
"VO₂max predicts how long you live" and "grip strength predicts longevity" are **observational associations**. The reels imply "train grip → live longer." Grip strength is a *proxy* for total-body strength/health/frailty, not a lever you crank for lifespan; nobody has RCT'd "squeeze harder, die later." The physicians and physios you're courting will pounce instantly — this is the textbook epidemiology error.
**Fix:** Hard voice law — **"marker of" ≠ "cause of."** Say: "Grip strength is one of the strongest *markers* of all-cause mortality (Leong, Lancet 2015) — it's a window into whole-body strength and frailty, not a magic lever." Then route to the trainable thing (total-body strength). Same discipline on VO₂max: it *is* more plausibly causal via cardiorespiratory fitness, but still say "improving fitness lowers risk" not "VO₂max buys you years."

**0.3 · Your entire moat is "the source is on screen," and your flagship citation names the wrong journal.**
"Mandsager, **JAMA** 2018" — it's **JAMA Network Open** (2018;1(6):e183605), not JAMA. One wrong journal name in a Citation Chip and the moat is gone — you've become The Confident Lie with a serif font. This will happen at scale unless the pipeline forbids it.
**Fix:** `citation-library.json` must carry **Author · Journal · Year · Volume/DOI**, each verified against the *primary source* (PubMed/DOI), never a secondary blog or a "studies show" recall. Add a publish gate: no Citation Chip renders unless the DOI resolves. Show the DOI on the card — that's the ultimate screenshot-proof flex, and nobody else does it.

## TIER 1 — Wrong, overstated, or internally inconsistent science (line-items)

**1.1 · "2% dehydration = strength loss" — overstated.** The robust evidence is for *endurance, thermoregulation, cognition, and RPE*. The maximal-strength effect is small (~2%, Savoie 2015 meta) and inconsistent. As written a scientist eye-rolls.
→ Fix: "2% body-mass dehydration clearly degrades endurance and thermoregulation (ACSM Position Stand, Sawka 2007); strength effects are smaller and less consistent."

**1.2 · "Leucine threshold ~20–40 g" — category error.** 20–40 g is the *protein per meal* dose to maximize MPS (~0.4 g/kg, Morton 2018). The *leucine* threshold is ~2.5–3 g. Conflating them is an instant tell to any nutrition scientist.
→ Fix: split them. "Protein per meal to max MPS ≈ 0.4 g/kg (~20–40 g); leucine trigger ≈ 2.5–3 g."

**1.3 · "Metabolism stable 20–60 (Pontzer)" — missing the size caveat.** True only *after adjusting for fat-free mass*. Unqualified, it contradicts every 45-year-old's lived experience and invites the "then why did I gain weight at 40?" dunk.
→ Fix (and it's a *better* hook): "Your cells don't slow with age — your muscle and daily movement do. Size-adjusted metabolism is flat from 20 to 60 (Pontzer, Science 2021)."

**1.4 · "Exercise = first-line antidepressant (Schuch 2016)" — medical overclaim.** "First-line" is clinical-prescribing language; guidelines position exercise as effective for mild–moderate depression / adjunctive, not universally first-line. A physician amplifier objects. Also Schuch 2016's effect shrinks after publication-bias correction.
→ Fix: use the stronger, current source — **Singh et al. 2023, BJSM umbrella review** — and say "exercise produces a moderate antidepressant effect, comparable to some first-line treatments," never "*the* first-line antidepressant."

**1.5 · Blood-gas barrier ships as BOTH 0.6 µm and 0.3 µm.** Section 1 says 0.6 µm; Section 8's worked example says 0.3 µm. Both are "right" (mean thickness vs thinnest gas-exchange side) but shipping both un-disambiguated is the exact screenshot-mockery you're trying to avoid.
→ Fix: pick one, label it. "Thinnest gas-exchange surface ≈ 0.3 µm (West)." Put it in the Numbers Registry as canonical so it can never drift.

**1.6 · Water-% reference bodies are internally inconsistent.** "60% water / 42 L" is a 70 kg *male* at 60%. But your One Reference Body is "70 kg M / 60 kg F," and women average ~50–55% body water — a 60 kg woman is ~31 L, not 42 L. If a woman does the math on "your body," it breaks. This directly sabotages the "MY body" self-relevance lever.
→ Fix: sex-specific numbers in the registry (M ~60%/42 L; F ~52%/~31 L), and frame all population numbers as "typical adult," letting the Body-Scan produce the *personal* figure.

**1.7 · "Newbie gains are neural not muscular (Sale 1988)" — dated dogma.** Damas 2016 (J Physiol) showed measurable hypertrophy earlier than the old model claimed (early "growth" is partly edema/damage, but growth is real sooner). Present the 1988 line as settled and a knowledgeable lifter flags you as behind.
→ Fix: "Early strength is *mostly* neural (Sale 1988), but real hypertrophy starts sooner than we used to think (Damas 2016)."

**1.8 · Creatine led as "cell hydration" — undersells and misleads.** Creatine's primary mechanism is phosphocreatine/ATP resynthesis (Kreider 2017). Cell volumization is secondary. Leading with hydration is the kind of half-truth the brand exists to kill.
→ Fix: "Creatine reloads your ATP energy system (phosphocreatine); the cell-water effect is a side note."

**1.9 · "Ice baths blunt hypertrophy (Roberts 2015)" — needs the context clause.** True for *chronic post-resistance-training* CWI; fine between competitive events or for a genuinely fried CNS. Absolute framing tells athletes to never ice, which is wrong.
→ Fix: "Ice baths right after lifting, done regularly, blunt muscle and strength gains (Roberts 2015) — save them for game-day recovery, not your growth block."

**1.10 · "Static stretch before lifting = myth" — outdated absolutism.** Consensus (Behm 2016): *long* holds (>45–60 s) transiently cut force/power; short holds (<30 s) in a warm-up are trivial. Banning all pre-lift stretching is itself a bro-myth.
→ Fix: "Long static holds before max effort cost you power; a short stretch in a warm-up is fine."

**1.11 · Adaptive thermogenesis sourced only to Biggest Loser (Fothergill 2016).** That's an extreme, rapid-loss outlier — generalizing it to normal dieters overstates the plateau. Skeptics know this study is contested.
→ Fix: pair with representative work (Rosenbaum & Leibel; Trexler 2014 review) and say "adaptation is real but usually a few hundred kcal, not the Biggest-Loser magnitude."

**1.12 · Small-n / narrow-population studies presented as law.** Nedeltcheva 2010 (n=10 crossover) and Milewski 2014 (adolescent athletes) are good hooks but must carry their limits. "Sleep <8h = 1.7× injury" is *adolescent athletes* — generalizing to a 42-year-old NRI exec is a stretch a peer will catch.
→ Fix: name population + n on the card; let the Confidence Tier do visible work (see 2.2).

**1.13 · Intestine "30 m² not a tennis court" — true but off-mission trivia.** Correct (Helander 2014) and a fun myth-bust, but it has zero training/nutrition decision attached. It's awe, not body literacy.
→ Fix: either tie it to something actionable (absorption capacity → why "you can only absorb 30 g protein per meal" is *also* a myth) or demote it to pure top-of-funnel, never the authority tier.

## The one-line gate to add to every brief
Before a claim ships it must pass: **(1)** primary source, correct journal, DOI resolves; **(2)** states "marker" vs "cause" correctly; **(3)** carries its population/effect-size/confidence tier; **(4)** has a Train-It turn — *what does the viewer do Monday because of this number?* Any flagship physiology "awe" fact that fails #4 is top-of-funnel reach content, not authority content, and must never wear the teaching-tier Citation Chip. Roughly half the current flagship hooks (20 nm synapse, 42 L water, 30 m² intestine, 0.3 µm barrier) fail #4 as written — reclassify them or give them a real behavioral turn.
